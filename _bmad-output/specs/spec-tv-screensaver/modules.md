# Modules and messages (CAP-5, CAP-6, CAP-7)

Built on the connection spec's framing (`<module>.<action>` requests with `rid`/`op`, `event {rev, module, kind, data}`, snapshot modules). First draft. Names may change while building, but the semantics hold.

| Module | Requests | Events / targeted frames | Owner only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `library` | `library.exists {sha256}`, `library.rename {id, name}`, `library.delete {id}` | `library.added`, `library.updated` (variants ready), `library.removed` | delete |
| `playlist` | `playlist.create/update {id?, name, items[], shuffle, imageSeconds, transition, kenBurns, muted}`, `playlist.delete {id}` | `playlist.changed`, `playlist.removed` | delete |
| `player` | `player.play {playlistId, index?}`, `player.next`, `player.prev`, `player.pause`, `player.resume`, `player.seek {positionMs}`, `player.setMuted {muted}` | `player.changed {playlistId, index, itemId, state, positionMs, startedAt, muted}` | — |
| `player` (display only) | `player.position {itemId, positionMs, durationMs?, at}` every 10 s and on change; `player.report {playlistId, index, itemId, state, positionMs, durationMs?, muted?, at}` on every (re)connect; `player.ended {itemId}` when the item played to its end | `position` updates the snapshot only, no `rev` bump. `report` is adopted and emits `player.changed`. `ended` makes the Pi start the next item (wraps to index 0) and answers `{moved: true}`; it answers `{moved: false}` for a stale item, a player without a playlist, or an emptied playlist (then the Pi also goes idle, emits `player.changed` and sends `display.control {action: stop}`). With `moved: false` the TV loops the item itself, or stops on `stop` | — |
| `settings` | `settings.update {…}` (cache cap, storage cap, streaming ceiling, default image seconds, transition) | `settings.changed` | yes |
| `display` (display only) | `display.capabilities {decoders[], hdr[], render, panel}` on every connect | stored per display | — |
| `display` (Pi → TV, targeted) | — | `display.load {itemId, variant, startAt, muted}`, `display.control {action: pause\|resume\|seek\|mute\|stop, positionMs?, muted?}`, `display.prefetch {itemIds[]}`, `display.pairing {qr, pin, expiresAt}` | — |
| `devices` | `hello {kind?: "phone"\|"display"}` (absent = phone; any other value is `err invalid`); rename, forget, `devices.forgetAll` (owner or display; also from the remote: hold Back 10 s; a controller gets `err forbidden`); `devices.approveDisplay {requestId}` (any paired phone; `not_found` once the request ended, `conflict` if the TV could not be admitted); `devices.startPairing` / `devices.cancelPairing` (display only) | from the connection spec; targeted: `devices.approvalRequested {requestId, name}` and `devices.approvalCancelled {requestId}` to phones (approved, timed out or the TV left), `display.pairing {qr, pin, expiresAt}` to the display on start, every rotation and after each pairing | forget, forget all |

Errors on this path: `unavailable` (phone pairs while no session is open, or the Pi has no LAN address for the QR), `timeout` (no phone approved the waiting TV in time, 120 s), `conflict` (a TV is already connected or waiting, or the 7-phone cap is reached), `invalid` (unknown `kind`).

- `display.load` starts an item (`startAt` is a position in milliseconds, `muted` the player's mute state). `display.control {action}` is `pause`, `resume` or `seek` (each with `positionMs`), `mute` (with `muted`) or `stop` (clear the picture; the Pi has nothing to play). `durationMs` (optional in `player.position` and `player.report`) lets the Pi clamp seeks; `muted` (in `display.load` and the optional field of `player.report`) is the player's mute state. `player.play`, `next`, `prev` and `ended` send `display.load`; `pause`, `resume`, `seek` and `setMuted` send `display.control`.
- `display` also has the event `display.changed {online}` (broadcast when the display connects or leaves) and the snapshot `{online}`; `player` has the snapshot `{playlistId, index, itemId, state, positionMs, startedAt, muted, at}` (state `idle|playing|paused`, `positionMs` is the position now). While playing, a phone's position is `piNow - startedAt`.
- Until story 4-4 the only playlist is `all`: the library's video items sorted by name ignoring case. Loose files in the Pi media directory become library items when the server starts, hourly and when the playlist is read (files changed in the last 10 s wait; a row whose file is gone is removed) (video: `mp4 m4v mov mkv webm ts`, any case; dot files, `*.bin` and other files are skipped). If the database cannot be read the last good list is used. An unknown playlist or an empty one gives `err not_found`; `next`, `prev`, `pause`, `resume` and `seek` with nothing playing also give `not_found`. A `seek` past the end clamps to the duration the TV reported (`durationMs`).
- While the display is away the player is paused at its last position (`player.changed` goes out). When the display reconnects, its `player.report` is adopted only if no phone or remote command arrived since it connected; otherwise the answer is `{adopted: false}` and the Pi sends `display.load` (plus `pause` if paused) or `display.control stop` so the TV shows what the Pi has.
- The TV sends one more `player.position` about 3 s after each load or seek, because the Pi drops positions for 2.5 s after its own command.
- A `player.position` the TV sent before a Pi-side seek, play or next cannot undo it (the Pi ignores TV positions for 2.5 s after those).
- Defaults: `muted: false` (video sound plays), `kenBurns: false`, `transition: crossfade`. Volume stays on the TV remote.
- `player.setMuted` overrides the playlist flag until the next `player.play`.
- `startedAt` and `at` are absolute TV/Pi times (connection spec clock rules), so phones show progress without polling.
- `player.*` commands from phones go to the Pi, which emits `player.changed` and sends `display.load` to the TV. With no display connected the Pi returns `err {code: unavailable}`.
- `player.*` from the TV remote go the same way and are owner-equivalent. Remote FF/RW maps to `player.seek` (±30 s); phones get a scrub bar. When the Pi is offline, the TV applies them locally and reports the result on reconnect (`architecture.md`).

## Library (story 4-1)

- Snapshot `{items[], storage: {usedBytes, capBytes}}`. An item is `{id, kind, name, sha256 (null until a loose file is hashed), bytes, width, height, durationMs, codec, hdr, fps, bitrate, createdAt}`. `usedBytes` counts stored items plus the declared size of unfinished uploads.
- `library.exists {sha256}` answers `{exists, id?}`. `library.rename {id, name}` (1-255 characters; `not_found` for an unknown id) emits `library.updated`. `library.delete {id}` (owner or display, because the display is owner-equivalent; the same rule applies to HTTP `DELETE /media/<id>`; `forbidden`/`403` for a controller, `not_found` for an unknown id) removes the file, its variants and the row, emits `library.removed {id}`, and a player that is on that item moves on as if it ended (idle with `display.control stop` if nothing is left). `library.added` carries the item; a finished upload (HTTP, see `media-server.md`) emits it. `library.updated` is also emitted when the background hash of a loose file finishes.
- Uploads and `DELETE /media/<id>` are HTTP, not module requests.

## Media worker (story 4-2)

- A library item also has `audioCodec`, `profile`, `state` (`new|ready|unsupported|error`), `stateReason`, `cacheRequired` and `variants` (the list of variants that exist: `poster`, `thumb` for video, `1920`, `thumb` for images). `state` is `new` until the worker has checked the item (and while no display has reported capabilities), `ready` when probed, checked and its variants exist, `unsupported` when the TV's `display.capabilities` cannot play it (`stateReason` says what to convert on the PC), `error` when a tool failed or hung.
- `library.updated` (the full item) is sent when the worker finishes an item and when a capabilities change flips its state. `unsupported` videos stay on disk, are left out of the `all` playlist and `player.play` on one gives `err invalid` with the reason.
- `variants` lists what exists. A client asks for `poster` (video) or `full` (image) when the wanted variant (`1920`, `thumb`) is not in the item's `variants` list or `GET /media/<id>/<variant>` answers `404` (no tools on the Pi, variant skipped because the source is not larger, job not done yet).
- `width` and `height` are the size as displayed: rotation and the sample aspect ratio (anamorphic video) are applied. `hdr` is `SDR`, `HDR10`, `HLG`, `DolbyVision` or, for Dolby Vision with a compatible base layer, `DolbyVision/HDR10` (also `/HLG`, `/SDR`); such a file plays on a TV that lists only the base format.
- The check also compares the video size with the decoder `max=` the TV lists (else its `panel`), and H.264 High 10 / 4:2:2 and HEVC Rext / 4:4:4 / 12-bit profiles with the listed `profiles=[...]`.
- A hand-copied loose file that would have been refused as `too_heavy` is kept and marked `unsupported` with the same reason. Without `ffprobe` on the Pi nothing is refused at upload (the check needs the bitrate); the worker enforces it once the tool exists. The quick probe on the last chunk waits 15 s at most; if it takes longer the upload is accepted and the worker enforces the limit later.
- `error` items are retried automatically (at most 3 times per run) at the hourly tool check and when the TV's capabilities change.
- A video above the streaming ceiling (`TVSAVER_STREAM_CEILING_MBPS`, default 80) is stored with `cacheRequired: true`. If it is also larger than 25 % of the TV cache cap (`TVSAVER_TV_CACHE_BYTES`, default 2.5 GB) the last upload chunk gets `422 {code: "too_heavy", reason}` and the file is deleted.
